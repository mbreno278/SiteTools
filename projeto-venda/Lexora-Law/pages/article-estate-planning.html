A Guide to Estate Planning for Modern Families

Estate planning is a process for documenting wishes, organizing assets, and helping trusted people act when needed.

By Lexora Editorial Team · Updated November 2026

Planning is not limited to older adults or people with substantial wealth. Many adults benefit from considering who should make decisions, how dependents should be supported, and how property should be managed if they become unable to act.

Depending on local law, planning may involve a will, trust, financial power of attorney, healthcare directive, beneficiary designations, and guardianship nominations where appropriate.

Consider core documents

Some assets pass according to beneficiary designations or ownership arrangements rather than a will. Review retirement accounts, insurance policies, and jointly held property as part of a coordinated plan.

Keep beneficiary information aligned

Marriage, separation, a new child, a move, a business sale, or a significant change in assets can be a reason to revisit existing documents.

Review after major life events

Make an inventory of key assets and obligations, identify trusted decision-makers, gather existing documents, and discuss your goals with an estate planning professional licensed in the relevant jurisdiction.

A thoughtful first step

  • Gather the relevant documents and facts.
  • Identify the jurisdiction and deadlines that may apply.
  • Ask a qualified professional to assess your specific circumstances.
General information can help you prepare better questions, but it cannot replace advice based on your individual facts and local law.
