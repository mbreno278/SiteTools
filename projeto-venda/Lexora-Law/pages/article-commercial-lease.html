What to Know Before Signing a Commercial Lease

A commercial lease can shape operating costs and flexibility for years. Review the full agreement, not only the headline rent.

By Lexora Editorial Team · Updated November 2026

Before signing, understand the complete financial commitment and the responsibilities allocated to landlord and tenant. Lease language can differ significantly across jurisdictions and property types.

Look beyond base rent. Ask how operating expenses, taxes, insurance, utilities, maintenance, common-area costs, and future increases are calculated and reconciled.

Understand the cost structure

Confirm that the permitted use fits the intended business. Review signage, improvements, accessibility, permits, assignment, subletting, and restoration obligations.

Review use and alteration rights

Consider renewal options, early termination rights, assignment provisions, default notices, cure periods, and what happens if the business outgrows the space.

Plan for change

What is the full estimated occupancy cost? Who is responsible for repairs and building systems? Are renewal terms clear? Can the space be assigned or sublet? What approvals are needed for improvements?

Questions to raise during review

  • Gather the relevant documents and facts.
  • Identify the jurisdiction and deadlines that may apply.
  • Ask a qualified professional to assess your specific circumstances.
General information can help you prepare better questions, but it cannot replace advice based on your individual facts and local law.
